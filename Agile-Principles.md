## Scrum vs Kanban

Scrum and Kanban are both Agile approaches, but they organise work differently.

Scrum uses fixed-length Sprints. The team plans a group of work at the beginning of each Sprint and works towards completing that work during the Sprint. Scrum also has defined events such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective.

Kanban focuses on continuous workflow. Instead of organising work into fixed Sprints, tasks move through different stages of a workflow. Kanban also uses work-in-progress limits to help control how much work is being done at the same time.

I think Scrum is useful when a team benefits from having a clear Sprint structure and regular planning and review points. Kanban can be more suitable when work needs to be prioritised and changed continuously.

## Most Useful Agile Principle

The Agile principle I think will be most useful in my work is:

**"Welcome changing requirements, even late in development."**

I think this is useful because requirements and priorities can change during software development. As an intern, I need to be able to adapt when my supervisor gives me new feedback or when the requirements of a task change.

Instead of being too focused on my original approach, I should listen to feedback, understand what needs to change, and adjust my work accordingly. This can help me become more flexible and work better with the team.
