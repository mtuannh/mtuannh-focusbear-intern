## Key roles in an Agile team

### Developer
Developers are responsible for building and improving the product. They work on assigned tasks, write and review code, fix issues, and work with other team members to deliver features.

### QA (Quality Assurance)
QA checks whether the product works as expected and helps identify bugs or issues before features are released.

### Product Manager (PM)
The Product Manager focuses on what the product should achieve. They help define requirements, priorities, and product direction based on user and business needs.

### Agile Project Manager (Agile PM)
The Agile Project Manager helps coordinate the team's work and makes sure the development process stays organised. They can also help track progress and remove blockers.

### Other supporting roles
Other roles such as UX designers, stakeholders, and customer support can provide information and feedback that helps the team understand user needs and improve the product.

## How do these roles collaborate in Kanban vs Scrum?

In both Kanban and Scrum, the different roles need to communicate and work together to move work from requirements to a completed product.

In Scrum, work is usually organised into fixed sprints, with regular events such as sprint planning, daily stand-ups, reviews, and retrospectives.

Kanban is more focused on continuous workflow. Work moves through different stages on a Kanban board, and the team focuses on managing the amount of work in progress.

## My role in the Agile team

As a Frontend Intern, my main responsibility is to work on frontend tasks and contribute to the existing Focus Bear product. I need to understand the requirements, make changes to the frontend, test my work, and communicate my progress or blockers with the team.

Daily stand-ups are also an important part of my role. I can use them to update the team on what I am working on, ask questions about my progress, and raise any blockers or unclear requirements.

## How my role interacts with other roles

My frontend work can interact with different parts of the team. For example, I may need clarification about a requirement before starting a task, or feedback from another team member after making a change.

I also need to communicate when I find a problem or when something is unclear instead of making assumptions. This helps avoid unnecessary rework and makes it easier for other team members to understand the current state of my work.

## How I can improve teamwork with another role

One role I may frequently work with is QA because frontend changes need to be checked to make sure they work correctly.

I can improve teamwork with QA by clearly explaining what I changed and mentioning anything that may need particular attention when the work is being checked. If a bug is found, I should understand the issue, ask questions if needed, and fix it rather than taking the feedback personally.

Good communication between frontend development and QA can make the testing and fixing process more straightforward.
