# Inclusive Design Reflection

## Who are considered vulnerable populations, and what challenges might they face in digital spaces?

Vulnerable populations can include people with disabilities, neurodivergent individuals, older people, and people who may have difficulties accessing or using digital products. In digital spaces, they may face problems such as confusing interfaces, too much information, difficult navigation, or content that is not designed around different needs.

For people with ADHD or Autism, too many notifications, complicated interfaces, unpredictable interactions, or too many things happening at once can make an app harder to use.

## What ethical considerations are important when designing or working with neurodivergent individuals?

I think the main consideration is to avoid assuming that everyone uses a product in the same way. The design should give users enough control and avoid creating unnecessary pressure or frustration.

It is also important to communicate respectfully and not treat neurodivergent users as if they need to be "fixed". The product should support them while still allowing them to choose what works best for them.

## How can you make interactions and content more accessible for people with ADHD or Autism?

The interface should be simple and predictable, with clear instructions and consistent navigation. Important information should be easy to find without making the user go through too many steps.

Notifications and reminders should also be useful rather than overwhelming. Users should have control over how often they receive them, because too many reminders can become another distraction.

## How can you support neurodivergent team members in a professional setting?

I can support neurodivergent teammates by communicating clearly and not assuming that everyone prefers the same working style. If I need something from a teammate, I should explain what I need and give them enough context.

I should also respect different communication styles and be open to using different ways of communicating when needed.

## How can you adjust your communication style to be more inclusive of neurodivergent users and teammates?

I can make my communication more direct and clear instead of relying on unclear hints or assumptions. When discussing a task, I should explain what needs to be done and ask questions if I am not sure that the other person understands.

I should also avoid sending unnecessary messages or information that could make a simple task more confusing.

## What are some common UX or communication pitfalls that might make Focus Bear less accessible or supportive?

One problem could be having too many notifications or reminders at the same time. Another problem could be making the interface too complicated or requiring users to go through too many steps to complete a simple action.

I also think unclear messages can make the experience more frustrating, especially when users are already having difficulty focusing. The product should make it clear what the user needs to do without giving them too much information at once.

## What is one practical change you can make in your work to better support vulnerable populations?

One practical change I can make is to think about accessibility when working on frontend features instead of only checking whether the feature technically works. I can pay more attention to whether the interface is clear, predictable, and easy to understand.

For example, if I work on a button, form, or notification, I can check whether the user can easily understand what it does and whether there is unnecessary information or interaction.

## First-person account reflection

I read a first-person account from someone with ADHD who had tried many productivity apps. One thing that stood out to me was that some productivity apps became overwhelming because they required too much setup, too many tasks, or too much organisation. The person described situations where managing the productivity system itself became another task.

This made me think differently about productivity apps. Adding more features does not always make an app more useful. For some users, especially people who already struggle with executive function, a complicated system can make it harder to start or complete a task.

This is relevant to Focus Bear because the product is designed to support focus and productivity. The interface should therefore help reduce distractions rather than create more things for the user to manage.

## Design improvement

One improvement I think Focus Bear could make is giving users more control over notifications and reminders in a simple way. Users could choose which reminders they want and how often they appear without having to go through a complicated setup.

This could make the app feel less overwhelming while still providing the external reminders that some users find helpful.

## Practising a clear and supportive response

If a teammate told me they were struggling to focus or were overwhelmed by a task, I would first listen and avoid making assumptions about why they were struggling. I would ask what part of the task they were having difficulty with and see whether I could help make the next step clearer.

I would try to be patient and supportive instead of telling them to simply work faster or focus more.
